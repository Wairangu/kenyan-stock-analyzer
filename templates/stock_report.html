📖 What These Terms Mean (Plain English)

P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)

How many shillings you pay for every 1 shilling of profit. Low P/E (<15) = potentially cheap. High P/E (>25) = investors expect fast growth. {% if fundamentals.pe_ratio %}
{{ interpret_pe(fundamentals.pe_ratio) }} {% endif %}

PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings to Growth)

P/E divided by earnings growth rate. PEG < 1.0 may mean the stock is undervalued relative to its growth. {% if fundamentals.peg_ratio %}
{{ interpret_peg(fundamentals.peg_ratio) }} {% endif %}

ROE (Return on Equity)

How much profit the company makes with shareholder money. 15%+ = good, 20%+ = excellent. {% if fundamentals.roe %}
{{ interpret_roe(fundamentals.roe) }} {% endif %}

ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)

How well the company uses ALL invested money (from shareholders AND lenders) to generate profit. ROIC > 15% is strong.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Momentum indicator from 0-100. Above 70 = overbought (price ran up fast, may pull back). Below 30 = oversold (price fell too far, may bounce). 30-70 = neutral. {% if latest.rsi %}
{{ interpret_rsi(latest.rsi) }} {% endif %}

MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)

Tracks the relationship between two moving averages. MACD above signal line = bullish. MACD below signal line = bearish. A "cross" signals a potential trend change.

Bollinger Bands

Price 'envelope' around a moving average. Price near upper band = relatively expensive. Price near lower band = relatively cheap. Bands widen when volatility increases.

Debt-to-Equity

How much debt vs. shareholder equity. Below 0.5 = conservative (low risk). Above 2.0 = aggressive (high risk). {% if fundamentals.debt_to_equity %}
{{ interpret_de(fundamentals.debt_to_equity) }} {% endif %}

Operating Margin

Percentage of revenue left after paying for operations. Higher = more efficient business. 20%+ is generally strong.

Net Margin

Percentage of revenue that becomes actual profit after ALL costs. The 'bottom line' — higher is better.

EPS Growth

How fast earnings per share are growing year-over-year. 10%+ is solid. 20%+ is excellent. Negative = profits shrinking.

Revenue Growth

How fast total sales are growing year-over-year. Growing revenue = expanding business. Falling = may be losing market share.

📚 Chart & Indicator Glossary — explained like you're 10

Every term and short-form used on this page, in plain words with a simple example.

TermWhat it means (simple)Example
RSI
(Relative Strength Index)		 A 0–100 speed meter of how fast the price has been rising or falling. Above 70 = bought too fast (may rest/drop); below 30 = sold too fast (may bounce). Like a swing pushed very high (70+) — it usually swings back down soon.
Stochastic
(%K and %D)		 Two lines (0–100) showing where today's price sits inside its recent high–low range. Above 80 = near the ceiling; below 20 = near the floor. %K is the fast line, %D is the slower, smoother line. A ball in a box: at 90 it's near the top (may come down), at 10 near the bottom (may bounce up).
MACD
(Moving Avg Convergence Divergence)		 Compares a fast and a slow average of the price. When the MACD line crosses above its signal line it's a bullish hint; crossing below is bearish. The bars show how far apart they are. Two runners: when the fast runner overtakes the slow one, momentum is turning up.
Bollinger Bands A price "envelope" — an average line with an upper and lower band. Price near the upper band = relatively expensive; near the lower = relatively cheap. Bands widen when the price is jumpy. Guard-rails on a road: touching the top rail means the price has run high for now.
SMA / EMA
(Simple / Exponential Moving Average)		 The average price over the last N days, drawn as a smooth line to reveal the trend. SMA 20 = 20-day average, SMA 50 = 50-day. EMA reacts a bit faster (weights recent days more). Price above its SMA 50 line = generally trending up.
Golden / Death Cross When the short average crosses above the long average = "golden cross" (bullish). Crossing below = "death cross" (bearish). Fast line jumping over the slow line going up = a hopeful sign.
Volume How many shares were traded that day — how busy the day was. Green bar = price rose that day, red bar = price fell. "Vol SMA 20" is the 20-day average, a baseline. A tall green bar on a price jump = lots of buyers piling in.
ATR
(Average True Range)		 A "bounciness" meter — how much the price typically moves in a day. Higher ATR = wilder swings; lower = calmer. ATR of 2 KES means the price usually moves about 2 shillings a day.
OBV
(On-Balance Volume)		 A running tug-of-war score: it adds volume on up days and subtracts it on down days. Rising OBV = buyers winning; falling = sellers winning. Like a scoreboard — if it keeps climbing, buyers are stronger.
Support & Resistance Support = a price "floor" where buyers tend to step in and the price bounces up. Resistance = a "ceiling" where sellers step in and the price stalls. If a stock keeps bouncing off 50 KES, that 50 is support (the floor).
TTM / YoY / FY TTM = "trailing twelve months" (the last year of results). YoY = "year over year" (vs the same time last year). FY = "financial year". "Revenue growth YoY +10%" = sales are 10% higher than a year ago.
